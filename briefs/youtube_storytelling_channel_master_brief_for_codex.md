## 1. Executive summary

This project is an English-language, long-form, faceless storytelling YouTube channel aimed primarily at the **USA**, then the **UK** and **Canada**.

The core format combines:
- the audience psychology of BOUBLI/SELKET-style “I thought I was finished / somehow I got out” personal stories,
- the brand consistency of a recognizable recurring character,
- and a different storyteller every episode so the channel does not become repetitive.

The concept must **not copy specific BOUBLI/SELKET stories, characters, or Mr. Ranks designs**. Only the high-level storytelling psychology and repeatable format are being adapted for a Western English-speaking audience.

### Core promise
> **One recognizable host. Different people. Crazy believable stories about moments when they thought they were completely finished.**

### Analyst launch verdict
**GO — High-potential test.**

Virality is not guaranteed. The concept has strong viral mechanics: immediate curiosity, first-person intimacy, tension + humor, escalating complications, strong payoff/twist, repeatability, recognizable branding, and a future audience-submission flywheel.

## 3. Core channel format — LOCKED

### Permanent host
There is **one permanent recurring main character/host** across the whole channel.

The host:
- is the recognizable face/brand,
- appears only at the beginning,
- delivers a **5–10 second hook**,
- introduces the storyteller,
- then **disappears completely**.

The host does not interrupt the story afterward.

Example:

**HOST**
> “Jake got pulled over at two in the morning. At first he wasn’t worried. Then his friend reminded him what was still inside the car. Jake… tell them what happened.”

**CUT TO JAKE**

**JAKE**
> “Man… even hearing somebody else say it like that makes my stomach turn…”

The host is finished.

---

## 4. Storyteller format

Every episode introduces a **different guest/storyteller**.

The storyteller:
- narrates in first person,
- feels like a real person telling friends what happened,
- uses pauses, nervous laughter, contradictions, self-corrections, humor, embarrassment, and emotion,
- carries the entire episode after the host hook.

Avoid documentary-style narration. Prefer conversational delivery.

Bad:
> “On June 15th, 2022, the subject departed the residence…”

Good:
> “So it’s like two in the morning, right? We’re driving home… and then I see blue lights behind us.”

---

## 5. Story psychology

We are not copying stories. We are adapting the psychological engine:

> **Normal situation → small problem → realization → escalation → “I’m finished” moment → unexpected change/twist → aftermath.**

The viewer should repeatedly ask:
- What is going to happen?
- Will they get caught?
- How can this get worse?
- What is the twist?
- How did they get out?
- What happened afterward?

Primary emotional blend:
**Tension + humor + relatability + relief**

This is not primarily a crime channel. Crime/police is only one pillar.

---

## 6. Long-form story structure

1. **Cold hook** — 5–10 seconds. Give danger/problem, not resolution.
2. **Normal life** — make storyteller relatable.
3. **Setup** — people, place, why they are there.
4. **First problem**
5. **Realization** — why situation is worse than it appears.
6. **Escalation** — 2–4 complications.
7. **Peak “I’m finished” moment**
8. **Twist/change**
9. **Release** — tension breaks.
10. **Human aftermath** — what changed?
11. **Final callback/joke/lesson**

Target duration initially:
**10–15 minutes**

Do not pad stories just to hit a duration target.
